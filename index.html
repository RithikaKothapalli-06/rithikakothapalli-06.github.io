01 · WarrousJun 2026 – present

Customer identity resolution

One car buyer can show up as five different people across sales, service, web leads and marketing lists. This pipeline decides who is who, for every tenant on the platform.

Problem
35M+ records from four sources with typos, missing fields and shared family phones. Exact-match rules either merged strangers or missed obvious duplicates.
Approach
Deterministic rules first, then Splink probabilistic matching with PyTorch name embeddings and an XGBoost scorer. Only the ambiguous middle band goes to an LLM resolver. GraphFrames connected components turn matched pairs into one golden record per customer.
Result
A unified Customer 360 that segmentation, marketing and the audience agent all read from.

02 · WarrousJun 2026 – present

QA business dashboard

Raw Allure output is written for test engineers. Product owners wanted to know three things: is the build healthy, what kind of failures are these, and who owns them.

Problem
Test results lived in JSON files that only the QA team could read, so status updates happened in meetings.
Approach
A Python generator parses Allure result JSON, classifies each failure as a product defect, test defect or environment issue, maps it to an owner, and writes a self-contained HTML dashboard.
Result
Wired into the Azure DevOps pipeline so the report rebuilds on every run, including the failed ones where it matters most.

03 · LakaryaFeb – Jun 2026

Operations proposal generator

A team of specialised agents that drafts an operations proposal from an RFP, grounded in the company's own past proposals.

Problem
Every proposal started from a blank page and a hunt through old documents for reusable scope, staffing and pricing language.
Approach
A planner splits the RFP into sections. A retriever pulls the closest historical proposals from a vector store on Azure. Section writers run in parallel, and a reviewer checks the draft for consistency before it is assembled.
Result
Analysis and reporting time per proposal fell by roughly 60%, and drafts stayed consistent with how the company actually bids.

Open-source AI agent platform

Research and architecture for moving a data team's AI work off a single managed vendor and onto open-source tools, with one shared foundation that many agents can build on.

Problem
Licence fees on every unit of compute, slow interactive answers from a batch-first engine, and AI helpers built one at a time with nothing shared.

Ask-your-data analytics assistant
